01

What Zyrex is, in copyright terms

Zyrex is a community catalogue and discussion platform for video editing. Members publish entries — leaks, plugins and audios — and the catalogue makes them searchable.

We are a host and an index, not a publisher. We don't commission the material, we don't license it to you, and we don't claim any rights over it. What we do operate is a working notice-and-takedown process: when a rights holder identifies something that shouldn't be here, we remove it and we don't let the same account keep re-uploading it.

This page covers copyright only. Anything else — a malicious file, a scam upload, harassment — is handled under the acceptable-use and security rules in our Terms.

02

Why you'll find leak material here

We want to be direct about this, because a policy that pretends otherwise helps nobody.

A large part of the catalogue is leak content: leaks, plugins and audios that members obtained and redistributed outside the channel their creator intended. Much of it was never meant to be free, and some of it is redistributed without the rightsholder's permission. Uploading that kind of material is a copyright risk carried by the member who uploads it, and removing it is our job the moment a valid notice arrives.

  • We don't verify licences before publication. We can't inspect every archive for redistribution rights, and we don't claim that we have.
  • We don't sell leaked material. Access is free; the site is supported by optional ad-gated passes.
  • We don't override creators. If you're the author or an authorised agent and you want an entry removed, a notice from you wins — no argument, no negotiation.
  • We keep records. Uploads are attributed to accounts, so a repeat pattern is visible to us and not just to you.

Uploaders carry the risk. Publishing a leak you don't have rights to is your decision and your liability. Zyrex will cooperate with valid notices and, where legally required, with the rightsholder — including identifying the account that uploaded the file.

03

Before you file

A few checks make a notice faster to action and harder to challenge:

  • Confirm it's actually yours. Owning a licence to use software is not the same as owning the copyright in it, and owning a copy of an entry doesn't let you police its distribution unless you're the author or an authorised agent.
  • Check whether it's licensed for redistribution. Some creators release work under permissive terms. If an entry credits the author and follows their licence, a takedown isn't the right tool — but tell us anyway if the credit is wrong.
  • Identify the exact entry. A page URL, a file name or a screenshot of the listing is far more useful than a description of the catalogue in general.
  • Contact the uploader first, if you're comfortable. Many members pull their own upload immediately when the original creator asks.

05

What a valid notice must contain

Include each of the following. An incomplete notice may be returned for correction instead of actioned, which only slows things down.

  • Your signature — physical or electronic, confirming the notice comes from you.
  • The work being infringed — title, version, registration or catalogue number, or a link to the original publication or store page.
  • The infringing material — the specific URL(s) on Zyrex and, where possible, the file name shown on the entry. One URL per line is ideal.
  • Your contact details — name, email and a postal address or telephone number.
  • Good-faith statement — that you believe the use isn't authorised by the owner, its agent, or the law.
  • Accuracy statement — that the information is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you're the owner or authorised to act for them.

Only the copyright owner or someone authorised to act on their behalf can file. If you're reporting for a studio or software company, say so and name the company.

06

What happens after you send it

  1. Receipt Your message lands in the copyright queue and gets an acknowledgement, usually within one business day.
  2. Review We check that every required element is present and that the URLs match live entries. Requests for clarification come back to you, not into a void.
  3. Removal Valid notices result in the entry being disabled or deleted, typically within 72 hours of a complete notice. Download links, previews, cached search results and any Discord mirrors we control are purged with it.
  4. Notification The uploader is told what was removed and why, and receives the notice so they can respond.
  5. Counter-notice window If the uploader files a counter-notice, it is forwarded to you. You then have the statutory window — typically 10 to 14 business days — to show you've started a court action.
  6. Outcome If no action is filed within that window, the entry may be restored. If action is filed, it stays down pending the case.

Bulk notices are fine. If you're a rightsholder clearing an entire catalogue, send one message with a structured list and we'll work through it in batches, reporting back which URLs were removed.

07

If your upload was removed

Removals are sometimes wrong: a licence was misread, a file was misidentified, or the claimant doesn't actually hold the rights. If that happened to you, send a counter-notice containing:

  • Your signature — physical or electronic.
  • Identification of the removed material — the URL or file name, and where it appeared before removal.
  • Statement under penalty of perjury — that you have a good-faith belief the removal resulted from mistake or misidentification.
  • Your name, address and telephone number.
  • Consent to jurisdiction — of the federal court district where you live (or, outside the U.S., any district where the operator may be found), plus agreement to accept service of process from the claimant.

We forward counter-notices to the original claimant. If they don't pursue legal action within the statutory window, the entry can be restored. Filing a false counter-notice carries the same consequences as filing a false notice.

A takedown is not a negotiation and not a verdict. If you genuinely believe you had the right to publish something, say so in a counter-notice rather than re-uploading it under a new name — re-uploads are removed on sight and count as a separate strike.

08

Repeat infringers

Accounts don't get unlimited attempts. Where a member is the subject of repeated valid notices, access is withdrawn in stages:

StageWhat happensUpload privileges
First noticeEntry removed, formal warning, guidance on what not to republishRetained
Second notice30-day upload suspension and account flagSuspended
Third noticePermanent removal of the account and all of its uploadsRevoked
Serious casesDeliberate catalogue-wide re-upload, monetised infringement, or ban evasion — no staged warningsRevoked immediately

Notices that were clearly erroneous, withdrawn by the claimant or overturned on counter-notice don't count toward the threshold. Neither do notices aimed at material you were licensed to publish.

09

Misrepresentation

Copyright law penalises abuse of this process in both directions. Anyone who knowingly and materially misrepresents that content is infringing — or that content was removed by mistake — can be held liable for damages, including costs and legal fees.

Don't use takedowns as leverage. Notices sent to silence criticism, win an argument or knock out a competitor's work are treated as abuse of process, and that ends access to Zyrex entirely.

10

Licensing, permissions and cooperation

Not every concern needs a takedown. If you're happy for your work to stay but want proper credit, a link back to your store, or your licence terms displayed on the entry, tell us — it's faster, and it keeps your work discoverable.

If you'd like your entire catalogue excluded from Zyrex going forward, send a short written request from an address or domain we can verify. We'll add it to a blocklist that new uploads are checked against, so takedowns become a one-time conversation instead of a monthly one.

Rights holders who contact us in good faith get a named human on the other end of the thread, a direct line for future notices, and — where a takedown is warranted — confirmation once the entry, its download links and its cached previews are gone.
